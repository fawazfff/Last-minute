FAQ

Questions you might have before trusting it with a deadline.

Short answers. No productivity jargon.

What is LastMinute?

LastMinute helps when you have too much work and not enough time. You tell it your deadline, how much time you can really work, and your tasks. It checks what fits and helps you make a smaller plan that has a real chance of being finished.

Is this just another to-do list?

No. A normal to-do list can organize 8 hours of work even when you only have 5 hours. LastMinute first checks whether the list is possible. If it is not, something must be reduced, moved, or dropped.

What does MUST mean?

It means this task protects the main result. If you skip it, the thing you are trying to finish may fail.

What does REDUCE mean?

Keep the useful part, but make the task smaller. For example, write a short useful README instead of spending an hour making a perfect one.

What does DEFER mean?

The task can be useful, but it does not need to happen before this deadline. Move it until later.

What does KILL mean?

Drop it for this deadline. This is for work that uses time without protecting the result you actually need.

Why does LastMinute keep a buffer?

Because estimates are rarely perfect. A task can take longer, your internet can fail, or something unexpected can happen. LastMinute keeps some time unplanned so one surprise does not destroy the whole plan.

What if I disagree with the triage?

You stay in control. You can move a task between Must, Reduce, Defer and Kill before you build the rescue plan.

What if a task takes longer than I expected?

Use the “Need more time” option while focusing on a task. LastMinute checks the plan again and can help move or cut less important work.

Does it use AI to make up my time estimates?

No. The important time and capacity calculations are normal deterministic math. The app does not get to pretend that 8 hours fits inside 5.

Do I need an account?

No. The hackathon version works without an account.

Where are my plans stored?

For this version, your plans are stored locally in your browser. They are not sent to a LastMinute account or database.

Who is LastMinute for?

Anyone facing a real deadline: students, developers, creators, freelancers, hackathon builders, or anyone who suddenly has more work than remaining time.
